### W1 & Q1: Sample-efficiency claim should be qualified more carefully

**Response**: We thank the reviewer for this important observation. We agree that fitting PCA on the *full source-domain training set* before evaluating smaller labeled subsets does not reflect a fully low-resource setting. While we observe the same sample-efficiency trend for both the PCA and No-PCA variants, we have revised **Section 5.5** to clarify that our experiments demonstrate the **sample efficiency of the linear probes**, given a fixed representation space. We also explicitly discuss this limitation in the **Limitations** section.

---

### W2: Cosine similarity between probes is suggestive, but it does not establish causality or universality.

**Response**: We fully agree with this limitation, which is why we already discuss it in the **Limitations** section. Our results are, at best, indicative of a shared latent space but **do not establish a causal relationship**. To further emphasize this point, we will also highlight it as an important direction for **future research** in the **Conclusion**.

---

### W3: The method relies on access to hidden states of a strong backbone model, which may limit deployment compared with black-box or probability-only detectors.

**Response:** While our probes require access to hidden states, the ablations in **Appendix D** show that detection performance remains competitive even on smaller backbone models, albeit lower than with larger models. Nevertheless, linear probing methods inherently require access to the hidden-state representations of the underlying language model and are therefore not directly applicable in black-box settings. We have added this point to the **Limitations** section.

---

### W4: It would be helpful to know how stable the findings are across substantially different model architectures and sizes.

**Response**: We thank the reviewer for this suggestion. A similar point was also raised by reviewer UQvR. We would like to highlight that **Appendix D** already includes **ablation studies** across different backbone models and model sizes. In response to the feedback from both reviewers, we have further extended these ablations by adding two additional model sizes, **Qwen-0.6B** and **Qwen-32B**. Please refer to our response to reviewer UQvR above for the detailed results.

### Q2: I would also suggest adding calibration/error analysis for false positives on human text.

**Revision:** This is an excellent suggestion that strengthens the practical implications of our work, thank you. To address it, we computed the **Expected Calibration Error (ECE)** for **both probing methods** across all **16 experimental settings**. Overall, our results show that both probes are **well calibrated** across datasets and, in several settings, achieve **better calibration than a fully fine-tuned RoBERTa model**. The table below reports the ECE results on DetectRL.

We emphasize that the calibration analysis is performed on the **projection scores** produced by the linear probes. This provides additional evidence that the probe outputs are well calibrated and supports their potential use for **fine-grained AI-edit detection**.
